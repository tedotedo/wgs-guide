Your Healthcare Professional Has Referred You for a Genomic Test

Your clinician has recommended whole genome sequencing (WGS) and has directed you to this website to help you prepare.

Before having the test, you need to understand what it involves and complete an NHS Record of Discussion form. This guide explains everything in plain English — what WGS is, what results can show, important things to be aware of (including unexpected findings and non-paternity), and your research choices.

🧬 Trio testing? If your child is the person being tested, the test works best when both parents also provide a DNA sample (called a "trio"). This means you may need to complete two forms — one for yourself (as a participant in the trio) and one on behalf of your child. You can do both on this website.

📄 When you're ready, you can complete each form online, download a PDF, and email it to your consultant. No data is collected by this website.

What to Expect

The Record of Discussion form covers three key areas. Here's a quick overview of what you'll be asked about.

1

Discussion Points

Seven key topics your healthcare professional will discuss with you, covering family implications, uncertainty, unexpected findings, DNA & data storage, health records, and research.

2

Research Choices

The NHS invites you to contribute to the National Genomic Research Library managed by Genomics England. You'll learn what this means and decide whether to take part.

3

Confirmation

Confirm your genomic test and research choices with your signature. Your healthcare professional will also complete their section of the form.
